Tax season often starts with a folder of PDFs and a quiet worry. Which page did that number come from? Is a form missing? And do you really want to hand all of it to a chatbot?

Every number, traced to its page.

This is taxtrace, running in your browser, on your own Mac. At the top, your to-do list, and the year's tax-saving moves, ranked by the dollars each one saves.

Further down are your documents, in a plain folder on your disk, sorted by tax year. Each one is tagged with the form it holds, and none of them has left your computer.

Behind the page, taxtrace reads each form box by box. Here is the W-2 from Initech: eight values read, and all five checks pass, including that box 4 is 6.2 percent of box 3. Every value keeps the page, and the exact text, it came from.

From those readings, taxtrace writes an entry sheet: everything your tax software will ask, in the order it asks. Each row is ready, an estimate, or missing, and a missing row names the command that fills it.

Last, the chat, for questions about tax rules and forms. Before anything goes to a cloud model, digits become pound signs and names and towns become stars, and the page shows exactly what was sent.

And a question with an amount in it never leaves your Mac. The model here answers it, and the page says why.
