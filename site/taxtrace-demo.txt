Tax season often starts with a folder of PDFs and a quiet worry. Which page did that number come from? Is a form missing? And do you really want to hand all of it to a chatbot?

Every number, traced to its page.

This is taxtrace, running in your browser, on your own Mac. At the top, what your workspace holds, then your to-do list, and the year's tax-saving moves, ranked by the dollars each one saves.

When your preparer sends more documents, open the Import tab and choose the files. taxtrace shows what it makes of each one: its form, its tax year and its owner. A passport, or a copy you already have, is skipped.

One button files the rest. The page then lists your documents by folder, sorted by tax year, each tagged with the form it holds, and none of them has left your computer.

Then the entry sheet: everything your tax software will ask, in the order it asks. Each value shows whether it is ready, an estimate or missing, and the document and page it was read from.

Switch to estimates and missing, and only the rows that need you are left. Each section links to the to-do items that mention it, so nothing waits unnoticed.

Last, the chat, for questions about tax rules and forms. Before anything goes to a cloud model, digits become pound signs and names and towns become stars, and the page shows exactly what was sent.

And a question with an amount in it never leaves your Mac. The model here answers it, and the page says why.
